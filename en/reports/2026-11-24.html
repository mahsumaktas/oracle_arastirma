Sections 01

Daily Summary

#

Agents are moving beyond writing code toward fixing PRs and making policy-controlled approval decisions. Who proposes, who authorizes and who verifies the outcome are becoming separate architectural choices. New evaluation guidance and privilege-separation research try to measure those distinctions. Real-video point tracking, proteomics optimization and ICU forecasting stand out, but benchmark improvements are not deployment or clinical validation. Editorial synthesis. Claude Ship · OPCP · SplitBench · RoboTrack · TWICE

Sections 14

AI-SCIENCE

#
  • E20 — MSRyzen: proteomics workflows with inspectable evidence. Natural-language prompts become DAGs, metric-guided parameter tuning and peak-level evidence/sequence-coverage views. Four public top-down datasets yield a reported 3.6–15.8% increase in proteoform identifications over defaults at 1% FDR. Why it matters: Automation remains connected to spectral evidence. Confidence: HIGH abstract/history, November 20 preprint. Limit: Dataset-specific identification metrics, not clinical benefit or biological validation of novel proteins. Paper
  • E21 — TWICE: good forecasts are not reliable treatment counterfactuals. A 79M model trained on one health system's ICU records achieves mean AUROC 0.836 across 19 outcomes versus 0.834 for the best compared supervised configuration. Yet opposite treatments can move forecasts like appropriate treatments on the values being corrected. Why it matters: An important negative control for digital-twin claims. Confidence: HIGH abstract/history, November 20 preprint. Limit: Single-system retrospective evaluation, not treatment advice, causal-effect validation or clinical deployment approval. Paper
Sections 15

INFRA

#
  • E12 — cuDSS 1.0: 151× is not the whole application. NVIDIA B200 versus Intel MKL 2026.1.0 yields geometric-mean analysis/factorization/triangular-solve speedups of 2.5×/5.9×/151×. New symmetric matching and Bunch–Kaufman pivoting target difficult systems' accuracy. Why it matters: Phase timing must accompany numerical robustness. Confidence: HIGH, November 23 vendor technical article. Limit: End-to-end speed depends on phase weights; not a guarantee for local hardware or every engineering problem. Source
  • E13 — Sirius core extension: DuckDB SQL with conditional GPU execution. Requires DuckDB 2.0; supported queries use GPUs, others fall back to CPU. Tuned two-GPU TPC-H-derived tests report 3.25–5.50× runtime gains. Why it matters: Acceleration without replacing the SQL interface. Confidence: HIGH, November 23 vendor/researcher guide. Limit: Pinned development builds; Sirius excludes result fetch/export while CPU timing includes CSV output. Query-time compute cost excludes startup/storage/S3/idle. Default two-GPU 10TB did not finish; tuning mattered. Not a general bill-savings guarantee. Source
  • E25 — Parallelizing the drafter while a speculative verifier waits. Layer-level draft states are accepted/corrected using hidden-state divergence, without retraining. Why it matters: Targets the drafting bottleneck. Confidence: HIGH abstract/history, November 20 preprint. Limit: The reported up to 1.65× applies to drafting, not total generation or service cost. Paper
Sections 16

SAFETY

#
  • E24 — SplitBench: executing tests and judging results are different privileges. Expected answers remain with a trusted grader, so taking over the test environment should not manufacture a pass. Authors report 180/202 task conversions, 98.7% native-grader agreement and 75% blocking of adversarial attempts. Why it matters: The verifier is itself an attack surface. Confidence: HIGH abstract/history, November 20 preprint. Limit: Some attacks remain; attributing many to original test weaknesses is not a blanket security guarantee. Paper
  • E26 — Reward integrity: did a red-team “win” violate the intended policy? Financial-guardrail experiments separately check required PII in attacks and actual policy violations in judge scores. An author-reviewed off-target set shows that an Opus judge can award false wins. Why it matters: Bad reward can masquerade as attacker progress. Confidence: HIGH abstract/history, November 20 preprint. Limit: Replay corpus, targeted cases and judge agreement, not universal model-accuracy rankings or production incident rates. Paper
